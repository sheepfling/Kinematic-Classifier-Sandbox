\begin{abstract}
This compendium joins the current workflow, classifier-ladder, and corpus-search notes into one structured reference. It is intended to be the broad technical front door for the repository: it explains how a study is declared, how a corpus is generated and audited, how evidence-provider families construct posteriors, and how promotion decisions are justified. The current 1D witness suite remains a proof of methodology layers rather than the final scientific endpoint. Its surrounding method vocabulary is closest to Bayesian filtering, reinforcement-learning-style search, and quality-diversity exploration rather than to a single monolithic classifier claim \cite{barshalom,bishop,sutton_barto,mouret_clune}.
\end{abstract}

\section{How To Read This Compendium}
This document is organized around three complementary questions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item How does a study move through the repository end to end?
  \item How do classifier and filter rungs construct evidence and earn promotion?
  \item How does the corpus layer generate, score, and defend a selected dataset?
\end{enumerate}

The three parts below intentionally preserve the companion-note split:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item the workflow part explains the study object and end-to-end flow,
  \item the ladder part explains evidence contracts and escalation logic,
  \item the corpus part explains candidate generation, adequacy, and selection.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope And Claims}
This compendium makes a methodological claim, not a final domain claim. The strongest current statement is:
\begin{quote}
\textit{The repository already demonstrates a reusable study workflow for kinematic classification in which corpus governance, evidence construction, posterior updates, and promotion decisions are made under explicit contracts.}
\end{quote}

That claim is intentionally narrower than any claim such as ``the final classifier architecture is complete'' or ``the current 1D witness suite is the target scientific benchmark.'' The current witness suite is still used as a methodology proof:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item they show how a study is declared,
  \item they show how evidence-provider families are compared under a shared posterior contract,
  \item they show how corpus adequacy can be scored and audited,
  \item they do not yet exhaust the space of higher-dimensional kinematic backends or final production corpora.
\end{itemize}

The generalization story therefore runs in one direction:
\begin{equation}
\text{1D witness success}
\Longrightarrow
\text{workflow and contract plausibility},
\end{equation}
but not yet
\begin{equation}
\text{1D witness success}
\Longrightarrow
\text{all future 3D studies are solved}.
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Corpus Explorer} refers to the generic corpus-generation, selection, and adequacy machinery.

\end{itemize}

\section{Generalization Path And Current Limits}
The repository is not intended to stop at a toy 1D classifier exercise. Its broader claim is that the \emph{contracts} should generalize even when the current witnesses remain simple. The main reusable contracts are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item the study object \(s = (\mathcal{D}, f, \mathcal{C}, m, \pi, b)\),
  \item the Corpus Explorer contract for generating, auditing, and selecting \(\mathcal{D}^{\star}\),
  \item the shared evidence-provider contract that emits \(\ell_t(c)\),
  \item the shared posterior update and study-level promotion decision.
\end{itemize}

Those contracts should survive several kinds of lift:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item from 1D trajectories to higher-dimensional kinematic state,
  \item from one hand-authored feature family to alternative feature dictionaries,
  \item from current witness classes to richer class taxonomies,
  \item from synthetic witnesses to more realistic or mixed-source corpora,
  \item from simple Gaussian evidence models to stronger nonlinear or mixed-mode backends.
\end{enumerate}

What does \emph{not} yet follow automatically is equally important:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item current 1D witness-suite success does not prove that every 3D backend is already solved,
  \item current feature-separation plots do not prove that any future class ontology will remain identifiable,
  \item current corpus audits do not remove the need for backend-specific validity and leakage checks after dimensional lift.
\end{itemize}

The practical reading is therefore:
\begin{equation}
\text{current repository status}
\approx
\text{methodology and contract proof}
\;+\;
\text{partial witness evidence},
\end{equation}
not yet
\begin{equation}
\text{final domain-complete classifier stack}.
\end{equation}

That is the correct standard for interpreting the repository today. If future work changes feature sets, class definitions, dimensionality, or sensing backends, the right question is not ``does the old toy still run?'' The right question is:
\begin{quote}
\textit{Do the same corpus, evidence, posterior, and promotion contracts still make the new study legible and auditable?}
\end{quote}
