\section{Problem Formulation}
The active repository goal is not only to maximize one benchmark score, but to prove that a study candidate can be declared, screened, executed, audited, and promoted through a reusable methodology surface. A study candidate combines a corpus, feature set, class set, classifier family, prior specification, and optional filtering backend.
Formally, the repo is solving a constrained study-selection problem rather than
a naked classifier-optimization problem. Let
\begin{equation}
s = (\mathcal{D}, f, \mathcal{C}, m, \pi, b)
\end{equation}
be a declared study. The desired outcome is not
\begin{equation}
\max_m \mathrm{Accuracy}(m),
\end{equation}
but rather
\begin{equation}
\max_{s \in \mathcal{S}}
U(s)
\quad
\text{subject to}
\quad
\mathcal{G}(s)=\mathrm{pass},
\end{equation}
where \(U(s)\) is a methodology utility and \(\mathcal{G}(s)\) is the joint
gate that enforces corpus adequacy, label validity, feature support, posterior
quality, and architectural compatibility.

A useful abstract decomposition is
\begin{align}
U(s)
&=
w_A A(s)
+ w_C C(s)
+ w_R R(s)
+ w_T T(s)
- w_F F(s),
\end{align}
where:
\begin{align}
A(s) &:= \text{task performance under the chosen metrics}, \\
C(s) &:= \text{calibration and posterior quality}, \\
R(s) &:= \text{robustness to priors, corruption, and sampling variation}, \\
T(s) &:= \text{transferability of the study logic to later lifts}, \\
F(s) &:= \text{fragility, leakage, and unsupported-claim penalties}.
\end{align}
The point of this decomposition is that the repo does not treat a study with
high endpoint accuracy but poor adequacy, high prior sensitivity, or obvious
leakage as a successful result.

The classification subproblem inside one study remains standard. For trajectory
\(\tau_i\) and time \(k\), the system emits
\begin{equation}
\hat{c}_{ik}
=
\arg\max_{c \in \mathcal{C}} p_{ik}(c),
\end{equation}
with posterior
\begin{equation}
p_{ik}(c)
=
p(c \mid y_{i,1:k}, s),
\end{equation}
but the study-level methodology question is broader:
\begin{quote}
\textit{when is this posterior history evidence for a trustworthy study claim,
rather than evidence only that one implementation can separate one curated
dataset?}
\end{quote}
That question is the reason the rest of the paper includes corpus, feature,
promotion, and transfer logic rather than only classifier equations.

\section{Canonical Repo Story}
The repository now has one intended front-door story:
\begin{quote}
\textit{This repository is a methodology workbench for kinematic classification studies. It declares a study candidate, generates or selects a corpus, validates class labels and feature excitation, runs evidence providers, updates posteriors under explicit priors, evaluates separability/calibration/confusion/adequacy, and assigns a promotion decision. The current 1D witness problems are controlled proofs of that machinery before 3D lift, including switching, nonlinear particle, RBPF, and mean-reverting stochastic witnesses.}
\end{quote}
The central study object is
\begin{equation}
s = (\mathcal{D}, f, \mathcal{C}, m, \pi, b),
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{D}\) is the selected corpus, \(f\) is the feature set, \(\mathcal{C}\) is the class set or class pair, \(m\) is the evidence-provider family, \(\pi\) is the prior regime, and \(b\) is an optional dynamics or filtering backend. The two generic engines are then
\begin{align}
\text{Corpus Explorer} &: (o, b) \mapsto \mathcal{D}^{\star}, \\
\text{Study Candidate Evaluator} &: (\mathcal{D}^{\star}, f, \mathcal{C}, m, \pi, b) \mapsto d,
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}
d \in \{\text{promote}, \text{revise}, \text{reject}, \text{defer}\}.
\end{equation}
This split is the coherence rule for the repository: the corpus side is responsible for producing a credible and auditable dataset, while the study-evaluator side is responsible for deciding whether a particular feature/class/classifier/prior combination is methodologically supported.

\section{Canonical Workflow Order}
The repository is easiest to understand when it is read as one ordered study
workflow rather than as a list of adjacent subsystems. The canonical order is:
\begin{center}
\texttt{Declare \(\rightarrow\) Analyze \(\rightarrow\) Generate \(\rightarrow\) Audit \(\rightarrow\) Evaluate \(\rightarrow\) Decide \(\rightarrow\) Package.}
\end{center}

In study notation, the workflow begins with a declared study object
\begin{equation}
s_0 = (\mathcal{C}, f, m, \pi, o, b),
\end{equation}
where the corpus itself has not yet been fixed. The later stages progressively
refine this declaration into an executed and judged study:
\begin{align}
s_0
&\rightarrow
g(s_0)
\rightarrow
\mathcal{D}^{\star}(s_0)
\rightarrow
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}(s_0)
\notag \\
&\rightarrow
R(s_0, \widehat{\mathcal{D}})
\rightarrow
d(s_0, \widehat{\mathcal{D}}, R)
\rightarrow
\mathcal{P}(s_0, \widehat{\mathcal{D}}, R, d),
\end{align}
where:
\begin{align}
g(s_0) &:= \text{feature/class geometry and static compatibility analysis}, \\
\mathcal{D}^{\star}(s_0) &:= \text{selected or generated candidate corpus}, \\
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}(s_0) &:= \text{audited corpus after validity and leakage gates}, \\
R(s_0, \widehat{\mathcal{D}}) &:= \text{evaluation records, posterior histories, and diagnostics}, \\
d(\cdot) &:= \text{promotion decision in } \{\text{promote}, \text{revise}, \text{reject}, \text{defer}\}, \\
\mathcal{P}(\cdot) &:= \text{packaged artifact bundle and report surface}.
\end{align}

This ordered view matters because the repository is not supposed to jump
directly from a trajectory generator to a classifier score. The intended claim
is stronger:
\begin{quote}
\textit{the study declaration, feature geometry, corpus adequacy, evidence
construction, posterior behavior, and final promotion decision should all be
mutually consistent and auditable.}
\end{quote}

\subsection{Stage 1: Declare}
The first stage defines the claim before any classifier is run. A declaration
chooses:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item a class set or class pair \(\mathcal{C}\),
  \item a feature family or feature set \(f\),
  \item a classifier or filtering family \(m\),
  \item a prior regime \(\pi\),
  \item a corpus objective \(o\),
  \item and an optional backend or dynamics family \(b\).
\end{itemize}
This is why the declared study object is not yet
\((\mathcal{D}, f, \mathcal{C}, m, \pi, b)\), but rather a study intent that
still requires corpus selection and validation.

\subsection{Stage 2: Analyze}
Before more trajectories are generated, the workflow inspects whether the
declared feature/class geometry is even promising. In abstract form, this stage
asks whether
\begin{equation}
g(s_0) =
\left(
\text{separability},
\text{overlap},
\text{dependency risk},
\text{oracle preview},
\text{prior fragility preview}
\right)
\end{equation}
is methodologically plausible. The main point is to distinguish two different
failure modes early:
\begin{align}
\text{classifier weakness}
\qquad\text{vs.}\qquad
\text{feature or class-definition weakness}.
\end{align}
This is the role of feature AUC, overlap, PCA, identifiability, and class-pair
inspection before the ladder is trusted.

\subsection{Stage 3: Generate}
Once the study declaration is geometrically plausible, the corpus side produces
or selects candidate datasets. The generic object is
\begin{equation}
\tau \sim p(\tau \mid \theta, c, b, \eta),
\end{equation}
where \(\theta\) are scenario variables, \(c \in \mathcal{C}\) is a class or
mode template, \(b\) is an optional backend, and \(\eta\) captures corruption,
stress, or sensor-regime settings. The selected corpus is then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}^{\star}
=
\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\mathcal{D}_k \in \mathfrak{D}(o,b)}
Q_{\mathrm{corpus}}(\mathcal{D}_k),
\end{equation}
with a score that rewards balance, boundary coverage, feature excitation, and
difficulty diversity while penalizing leakage, invalidity, and triviality.

\subsection{Stage 4: Audit}
The workflow refuses to treat \(\mathcal{D}^{\star}\) as trustworthy until it
passes explicit gates. In compact form,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
=
\mathcal{D}^{\star}
\;\text{such that}\;
\bigwedge_{j=1}^{J} G_j(\mathcal{D}^{\star}) = \mathrm{pass},
\end{equation}
where the gates include class validity, balance, coverage, feature excitation,
boundary support, and leakage. This stage is upstream of classifier claims:
\begin{equation}
\text{good classifier metrics on a leaky corpus}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{good study}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 5: Evaluate}
Only after the audited corpus exists does the classifier ladder run. The
generic evidence-provider contract emits
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}(y_k, \mathcal{H}_{k-1}, c) \rightarrow \ell_k(c),
\end{equation}
and the shared posterior contract then updates
\begin{equation}
p_k(c) \propto p_{k-1}(c)\exp(\ell_k(c)).
\end{equation}
This stage includes both static and dynamic evaluation:
\begin{align}
\text{static: }&
\text{feature overlap, oracle separability, pair hardness}, \\
\text{dynamic: }&
\text{posterior quality, confusion, calibration, prior sensitivity, robustness}.
\end{align}
The key design principle is that methods are compared through the same
evaluation surfaces even when their evidence atoms differ.

\subsection{Stage 6: Decide}
The decision layer integrates corpus, feature, and posterior evidence instead
of overtrusting any one artifact. In compact form,
\begin{equation}
d
=
\Delta(
g(s_0),
\widehat{\mathcal{D}},
R,
\text{fragility},
\text{robustness}
),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
d \in \{\text{promote}, \text{revise}, \text{reject}, \text{defer}\}.
\end{equation}
This is the layer that makes the repository a methodology lab rather than a
plot factory.

\subsection{Stage 7: Package}
The final stage is not mathematically deep, but it is methodologically
important. The study is only reusable if it can be inspected after the run.
The packaged bundle
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{P}(s_0, \widehat{\mathcal{D}}, R, d)
\end{equation}
contains the declaration, feature geometry, corpus audit, ladder evaluation,
and decision report in a stable artifact family. That packaging step is how the
repo turns math and code into a durable evidence record.

\section{Five Methodology Pillars}
The current repository should be read through five stable pillars:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Study Candidate Evaluator}: the generic engine for \(s = (\mathcal{D}, f, \mathcal{C}, m, \pi, b)\), static screening, Monte Carlo confirmation, prior sensitivity, separability, classifier quality, and final promotion;
  \item \textbf{Corpus Explorer}: the generic engine for corpus objectives, backend adapters, candidate samplers, adequacy/leakage/class-validity scoring, and selected-corpus generation;
  \item \textbf{Classifier / Filter Ladder}: the evidence-provider ladder from pointwise likelihoods through transition-aware accumulation and advanced-filter gates;
  \item \textbf{Evaluation / Promotion Layer}: the shared interpretation contract for adequacy, separability, calibration, prior sensitivity, confusion, and validation-ladder decisions;
  \item \textbf{1D Witness Suite}: the current small controlled problems that prove methodology layers before the framework is lifted to 3D geometry and richer dynamics.

These pillars are not parallel essays. They are compositional surfaces that all feed the same study decision.

\paragraph{Section-Level Symbol Policy.}
The global glossary is necessary but not sufficient once the paper becomes
mathematically dense. The manuscript is easiest to audit when each technical
section also begins with a \emph{local symbol table} that lists only the
symbols introduced, overloaded, or used in a section-specific way. In practice,
that means each major algorithm, feature, promotion, or contract section should
open with a short three-column table:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item the symbol,
  \item its meaning in that section,
  \item and a brief note on shape, scope, or interpretation.
\end{enumerate}
The global glossary then answers ``what does this symbol mean anywhere in the
paper?'' while the local table answers ``what exactly is this symbol doing
here?''.

The most important disambiguation is between raw observations, derived features, and evidence-provider inputs:
\begin{align}
z_k &= \text{raw observation at time } t_k, \\
\phi_k &= \Phi(z_{1:k}, t_{1:k}) = \text{feature or evidence summary derived from history}, \\
y_k &=
\begin{cases}
z_k, & \text{pointwise observation classifier}, \\
\phi_k, & \text{windowed or feature classifier}, \\
\nu_{k,c}, & \text{Kalman innovation evidence for class } c, \\
(\phi_k, \nu_{k,c}), & \text{hybrid or future combined evidence providers}.
\end{cases}
\end{align}
This convention lets one posterior section cover pointwise baselines, windowed summaries, sequential Bayes accumulation, Kalman innovation evidence, and transition-aware accumulation without pretending they all consume the same primitive signal.

The same symbols also define the repo's layer boundaries. A corpus produces
trajectory and metadata records,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}
=
\left\{
    (\tau_i, c_i^\star, s_i, m_i)
\right\}_{i=1}^N,
\end{equation}
where \(\tau_i\) is a trajectory, \(c_i^\star\) is the truth class, \(s_i\) is
scenario metadata, and \(m_i\) collects corruption, backend, and provenance
tags. A feature layer maps
\begin{equation}
(\tau_i, s_i, m_i) \mapsto \Phi_i,
\end{equation}
an evidence provider maps
\begin{equation}
(\Phi_i \text{ or } z_i \text{ or } \nu_i, \mathcal{H}_{k-1}) \mapsto \ell_k(c),
\end{equation}
and the posterior layer maps
\begin{equation}
(\pi, \ell_{1:k}) \mapsto p_k(c).
\end{equation}
This decomposition matters because the methodology claim is precisely that each
layer can change while the surrounding contracts remain stable.

\subsection*{Time, History, And Window Semantics}
The repository also needs explicit time semantics because irregular sampling is
one of the reasons simple feature choices can fail. For trajectory \(i\), let
\begin{equation}
\Delta t_{ik} = t_{ik} - t_{i,k-1}.
\end{equation}
A sample-count window of width \(w\) is
\begin{equation}
W^{\mathrm{count}}_{ik}(w) = \{k-w+1,\dots,k\},
\end{equation}
while a duration window of span \(T_w\) is
\begin{equation}
W^{\mathrm{time}}_{ik}(T_w)
=
\{j \le k : t_{ik} - t_{ij} \le T_w\}.
\end{equation}
This distinction is not cosmetic. If sampling is irregular, then
\begin{equation}
|W^{\mathrm{count}}_{ik}(w)| = w
\qquad \text{does not imply} \qquad
t_{ik} - t_{i,k-w+1} \approx \text{constant}.
\end{equation}
That is why the later feature-taxonomy and corpus-audit sections track both
sample-count sensitivity and duration sensitivity.

\sectionsymbol{\(p_{ik}, v_{ik}, a_{ik}, \theta_i\)}{position, velocity, acceleration, and class-conditioned motion parameters}{latent kinematic quantities}
\sectionsymbol{\(W_k, z_{i,1:k}, z_{i,W_k}, z_{i,k-1}, z_{i,k-2}\)}{active feature window and observation slices used by feature families}{windowed and derivative feature inputs}
\sectionsymbol{\(h(\cdot), g(\cdot), f_{\theta_i,c_i}(\cdot)\)}{measurement map, generic feature helper, and class-conditioned motion law}{model and feature maps}
\sectionsymbol{\(\phi, S\)}{generic feature placeholder and residual-scale matrix used in residual norms}{shorthand appearing in geometry and residual examples}
}

\noindent
For the current scalar witness problems, one trajectory is
\begin{equation}
\tau_i = \{(t_{ik}, z_{ik})\}_{k=1}^{n_i},
\end{equation}
with latent state
\begin{equation}
x_{ik} =
\begin{bmatrix}
p_{ik} \\
v_{ik} \\
a_{ik}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
z_{ik} = h(x_{ik}) + \epsilon_{ik}.
\end{equation}
The underlying continuous-time intent is that there exists a latent process
\(x_i(t)\) and an observation process \(z_i(t)\) such that the stored samples
are evaluations at \(t_{ik}\). The current 1D witness stack usually assumes
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{ik} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R_{ik}),
\qquad
x_{i,k+1} = f_{\theta_i,c_i}(x_{ik}, \Delta t_{i,k+1}) + w_{i,k+1},
\end{equation}
with class-conditioned motion law \(f_{\theta_i,c_i}\) and process noise
\(w_{ik}\). Even when no explicit filter is used, this latent-state notation is
still useful because it makes clear which later features are geometric summaries
of observations and which are approximations to latent kinematic quantities.

For the scalar 1D witnesses, the most common measurement map is
\begin{equation}
h(x_{ik}) = p_{ik},
\end{equation}
so direct velocity and acceleration are not observed. They are inferred either
through finite-difference features or through a state estimator. This is one of
the main reasons the ladder has both engineered-feature and model-based rungs.

The current feature pipeline then exposes
\begin{equation}
\phi_{ik} = \Phi(z_{i,1:k}, t_{i,1:k}),
\end{equation}
where \(\Phi\) may produce instantaneous, fixed-window, cumulative, robust-summary, derivative-based, or model-residual evidence.
More explicitly, the feature layer can be decomposed as
\begin{equation}
\Phi
=
(\Phi^{\mathrm{inst}},
\Phi^{\mathrm{window}},
\Phi^{\mathrm{cum}},
\Phi^{\mathrm{robust}},
\Phi^{\mathrm{deriv}},
\Phi^{\mathrm{resid}}),
\end{equation}
with each component mapping a trajectory prefix into a feature subvector.
Examples include
\begin{align}
\Phi^{\mathrm{inst}}_{ik} &= z_{ik}, \\
\Phi^{\mathrm{window}}_{ik} &= \bigl(\max_{j \in W_k} z_{ij},\ \min_{j \in W_k} z_{ij},\ \mathrm{range}(z_{i,W_k}),\ \mathrm{slope}(z_{i,W_k})\bigr), \\
\Phi^{\mathrm{cum}}_{ik} &= \sum_{j=1}^k g(z_{ij}, \Delta t_{ij}), \\
\Phi^{\mathrm{deriv}}_{ik} &= \left(\frac{z_{ik}-z_{i,k-1}}{\Delta t_{ik}},\ \frac{z_{ik}-2z_{i,k-1}+z_{i,k-2}}{\Delta t_{ik}^2}\right), \\
\Phi^{\mathrm{resid}}_{ik} &= \nu_{ik,c} \text{ or } \|\nu_{ik,c}\|_{S^{-1}}.
\end{align}
This decomposition makes later feature-taxonomy claims concrete: every feature
family corresponds to a distinct type of history use and a distinct failure
mode under noise, duration variation, or irregular sampling.

\subsection*{Class-Conditional Evidence Geometry}
The feature layer is not only a convenience transformation. It defines the
geometry of the classification problem. For class \(c\), the methodology
implicitly studies the class-conditional feature distribution
\begin{equation}
\phi_{ik} \mid c \sim p_c(\phi),
\end{equation}
and the class-pair geometry is then about the overlap structure of
\(\{p_a, p_b\}\). In the Gaussian approximation used by several diagnostics,
\begin{equation}
\phi \mid c \approx \mathcal{N}(\mu_c, \Sigma_c),
\end{equation}
so separation can be summarized by distances, overlaps, and principal axes.
That is why PCA, oracle AUC, and overlap studies belong upstream of classifier
promotion: they characterize the evidence geometry before one commits to a
specific recursive combiner.

\section{Stage V Evaluate: Bayesian Evidence Model}
The repository treats posterior updating as a reusable evidence-provider contract. Let
\paragraph{Section Symbols.}
\sectionsymbols{
\sectionsymbol{\(\ell_k(c)\)}{log evidence increment for class \(c\) at step \(k\)}{section-local evidence atom}
\sectionsymbol{\(\mathcal{H}_{k-1}\)}{history available before the current update}{may be observations, features, or residuals}
\sectionsymbol{\(\tilde{r}_k(c)\)}{unnormalized log posterior score}{pre-normalization quantity}
\sectionsymbol{\(Z_k\)}{normalization constant}{shared across classes at step \(k\)}
\sectionsymbol{\(\lambda_k^{ab}\)}{log odds for class \(a\) versus \(b\)}{used for prior-flip reasoning}
\sectionsymbol{\(y_k, \phi_k, \nu_{k,c}\)}{raw, feature, and innovation evidence atoms}{classifier-specific evidence inputs}
\sectionsymbol{\(p_{k-1}(c), T_{jc}, \pi(c)\)}{previous posterior, transition weight, and prior probability}{context for static and switching updates}
}

\noindent
The repository treats posterior updating as a reusable evidence-provider contract. Let
\begin{equation}
\ell_k(c) = \log p(y_k \mid c, \mathcal{H}_{k-1}),
\qquad
\mathcal{H}_{k-1} = \{y_1,\dots,y_{k-1}\}.
\end{equation}
The generic log-domain recursion is then
\begin{align}
\tilde{r}_k(c) &= \log p_{k-1}(c) + \ell_k(c), \\
\log Z_k &= \log \sum_{c' \in \mathcal{C}} \exp(\tilde{r}_k(c')), \\
\log p_k(c) &= \tilde{r}_k(c) - \log Z_k, \\
p_k(c) &= \exp(\log p_k(c)).
\end{align}
This is clearer than writing only \(p(y_k \mid c)\), because the implemented classifiers provide different evidence terms:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{lXX}
\toprule
Classifier & Evidence input \(y_k\) & Evidence term \(\ell_k(c)\) \\
\midrule
pointwise & current observation or local feature & \(\log p(y_k \mid c)\) \\
windowed & fixed-window feature vector \(\phi_k\) & \(\log p(\phi_k \mid c)\) \\
sequential Bayes & recursively emitted evidence stream & accumulated per-step \(\ell_k(c)\) \\
Kalman bank & innovation \(\nu_{k,c}\) & \(\log \mathcal{N}(\nu_{k,c}; 0, S_{k,c})\) \\
transition model & evidence plus class transition prior & \(\ell_k(c) + \log \sum_j T_{jc}p_{k-1}(j)\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Read literally, the families differ in what they treat as the evidence atom:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{pointwise}: one fresh observation or local feature is enough to score the class;
  \item \textbf{windowed}: a short feature vector \(\phi_k\) is the evidence atom, so the method can use local history without a full state model;
  \item \textbf{sequential Bayes}: the evidence stream itself is the object being accumulated, so each step contributes an incremental log-likelihood;
  \item \textbf{Kalman bank}: the innovation residual \(\nu_{k,c}\) is the evidence atom, and the class score is the Gaussian log density of that residual under the class-conditioned dynamics;
  \item \textbf{transition model}: the evidence update is preceded by a class-transition propagation step, so the method can represent switching behavior rather than only static identity.
\end{itemize}
That is the practical answer to the \(y_k\) notation question: \(y_k\) is the newest sample for pointwise scoring, a feature vector for windowed scoring, a per-step evidence stream for the accumulator, the innovation residual for the Kalman bank, and a transition-propagated class state for the switching model.

For the two-class case \((a,b)\), the log-odds form is
\begin{equation}
\lambda_k^{ab}
=
\log \frac{p_k(a)}{p_k(b)}
=
\log \frac{\pi(a)}{\pi(b)}
+ \sum_{i=1}^k \log \frac{p(y_i \mid a)}{p(y_i \mid b)}.
\end{equation}
Decision flip thresholds therefore follow directly: the decision changes from \(a\) to \(b\) when \(\lambda_k^{ab} < 0\), so the required prior shift is
\begin{equation}
\log \frac{\pi(b)}{\pi(a)}
>
\sum_{i=1}^k \log \frac{p(y_i \mid a)}{p(y_i \mid b)}.
\end{equation}

One worked arithmetic step from the current walkthrough table is:
\begin{align}
p_0(a) &= 0.5, \qquad p_0(b) = 0.5, \\
\Delta \lambda_1^{ab} &= \log \frac{p(y_1 \mid a)}{p(y_1 \mid b)} = -0.143, \\
\lambda_1^{ab} &= 0 + (-0.143) = -0.143, \\
p_1(a) &= \frac{1}{1+\exp(-\lambda_1^{ab})} = \frac{1}{1+\exp(0.143)} \approx 0.464.
\end{align}
This matches the promoted walkthrough table and makes the update numerically reproducible without inspecting the implementation.

\section{Stage V Evaluate: Transition-Aware Posterior Update}
\paragraph{Section Symbols.}
\sectionsymbols{
\sectionsymbol{\(\bar{p}_k(c)\)}{predicted class probability before seeing step \(k\)}{transition-propagated prior}
\sectionsymbol{\(T_{c'c}\)}{class transition probability from \(c'\) to \(c\)}{switching model parameter}
\sectionsymbol{\(p_k(c)\)}{updated posterior after the evidence step}{shared class-belief state}
\sectionsymbol{\(\mathcal{C}\)}{active class set}{summation domain for transitions}
}

\noindent
The ordinary Bayesian accumulator is the special case where class identity is assumed static. The transition-aware accumulator instead predicts the next class distribution before applying observation evidence:
\begin{align}
\bar{p}_k(c)
&=
\sum_{c' \in \mathcal{C}} T_{c'c} p_{k-1}(c'), \\
p_k(c)
&=
\frac{p(y_k \mid c)\bar{p}_k(c)}{\sum_j p(y_k \mid j)\bar{p}_k(j)},
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
T_{c'c} = p(c_k = c \mid c_{k-1} = c').
\end{equation}
If \(T\) is the identity matrix, the update reduces to the static-class accumulator. This is the mathematical reason the switching diagnostics figure is a real bridge artifact rather than just a plot of another heuristic score.

\section{Stage V Evaluate: Feature Likelihoods And Dependence Warnings}
When the evidence provider consumes feature vectors, the current math should be read as a likelihood over \(\phi_k\), not over raw measurements:
\paragraph{Section Symbols.}
\sectionsymbols{
\sectionsymbol{\(\phi_k\)}{feature vector at step \(k\)}{engineered evidence summary}
\sectionsymbol{\(\mu_c, \Sigma_c\)}{class-conditional feature mean and covariance}{feature-distribution parameters}
\sectionsymbol{\(d_\phi\)}{feature dimension}{size of the active feature set}
\sectionsymbol{\(p(\phi_k \mid c)\)}{feature likelihood under class \(c\)}{may be full-covariance or factorized}
}

\noindent
When the evidence provider consumes feature vectors, the current math should be read as a likelihood over \(\phi_k\), not over raw measurements:
\begin{equation}
\phi_k \mid c \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_c, \Sigma_c),
\end{equation}
which yields the Gaussian log-likelihood
\begin{equation}
\ell_k(c)
=
-\frac{1}{2}
\left[
(\phi_k-\mu_c)^\top \Sigma_c^{-1} (\phi_k-\mu_c)
+
\log |\Sigma_c|
+
d_\phi \log(2\pi)
\right].
\end{equation}
A diagonal or naive factorization may be written as
\begin{equation}
p(\phi_k \mid c) \approx \prod_j p(\phi_{k,j} \mid c),
\end{equation}
but this approximation is only reasonable when the features are low-dependency evidence channels.

A critical warning for cumulative features is
\begin{equation}
\phi_k^{\mathrm{cum}} = g(z_{1:k}),
\end{equation}
so treating
\begin{equation}
p(\phi_1,\phi_2,\dots,\phi_k \mid c)
=
\prod_i p(\phi_i \mid c)
\end{equation}
as independent is generally invalid because each \(\phi_i^{\mathrm{cum}}\) already contains earlier signal. Safe uses are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item use cumulative features only as final-trajectory summaries,
  \item use fixed-window features for approximate local evidence,
  \item use explicit sequence models for correlated evidence,
  \item use innovations or residuals when sequential evidence should remain additive,
  \item keep \texttt{history\_behavior} metadata in the feature manifest.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Stage V Evaluate: Kalman-Bank Innovation Likelihood}
\paragraph{Section Symbols.}
\sectionsymbols{
\sectionsymbol{\(F_c, H_c\)}{class-conditioned state and measurement operators}{dynamics and observation maps}
\sectionsymbol{\(Q_c, R_c\)}{process and measurement noise covariances}{uncertainty models for class \(c\)}
\sectionsymbol{\(\nu_{k,c}\)}{innovation residual for class \(c\)}{observation surprise under the predicted state}
\sectionsymbol{\(S_{k,c}\)}{innovation covariance for class \(c\)}{normalizer for Mahalanobis evidence}
\sectionsymbol{\(\hat{x}_{k|k-1,c}, P_{k|k-1,c}\)}{predicted state mean and covariance}{filter prediction outputs before update}
\sectionsymbol{\(x_k, x_{k-1}, w_k, v_k\)}{state, previous state, process noise, and measurement noise}{state-space model primitives}
\sectionsymbol{\(\Delta t_k, d_z\)}{sample spacing and observation dimension}{timing and normalization terms}
}

\noindent
The Kalman bank is the clearest example of model-based evidence rather than feature-summary evidence. For class \(c\), define
\begin{align}
x_k &= F_c(\Delta t_k)x_{k-1} + w_k, \qquad w_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q_c), \\
z_k &= H_c x_k + v_k, \qquad v_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R_c).
\end{align}
Prediction is
\begin{align}
\hat{x}_{k|k-1,c} &= F_c \hat{x}_{k-1|k-1,c}, \\
P_{k|k-1,c} &= F_c P_{k-1|k-1,c} F_c^\top + Q_c.
\end{align}
Innovation evidence is then
\begin{align}
\nu_{k,c} &= z_k - H_c \hat{x}_{k|k-1,c}, \\
S_{k,c} &= H_c P_{k|k-1,c} H_c^\top + R_c,
\end{align}
with class-conditioned log evidence
\begin{equation}
\ell_k(c)
=
\log \mathcal{N}(\nu_{k,c}; 0, S_{k,c})
=
-\frac{1}{2}
\left[
\nu_{k,c}^\top S_{k,c}^{-1} \nu_{k,c}
+
\log |S_{k,c}|
+
d_z \log(2\pi)
\right].
\end{equation}
Posterior recursion then reuses the same log-sum-exp normalization from the generic evidence contract. This is the main mathematical distinction between the Kalman bank and the purely feature-based classifiers.

\section{Stage II Analyze: Feature Taxonomy}
The methodology distinguishes feature families because they make different
claims about history, geometry, and transferability. A feature is not only a
column name. It is a declared mapping
\paragraph{Section Symbols.}
\sectionsymbols{
\sectionsymbol{\(f_j\)}{individual feature extractor \(j\)}{maps trajectory context to one feature output}
\sectionsymbol{\(\mathcal{F}\)}{full feature family universe}{partitioned into instantaneous, windowed, cumulative, robust, derivative, and residual sets}
\sectionsymbol{\(\mathcal{W}\)}{history window descriptor}{sample-count or duration based}
\sectionsymbol{\(\sigma(f_j)\)}{feature sensitivity vector}{summarizes nuisance sensitivity}
\sectionsymbol{\(T_{1\mathrm{D}\rightarrow 3\mathrm{D}}(f_j)\)}{transfer class of a feature principle}{direct, projected, model-specific, or rewrite-required}
\sectionsymbol{\(\hat{u}, \hat{z}_{t|t-1}, \nu_t\)}{proxy derivative signal, model prediction, and residual input}{model-aware feature ingredients}
\sectionsymbol{\(\mathbb{R}^{d_j}, d_j, \tau\)}{feature codomain, feature output dimension, and excitation threshold}{feature-shape metadata}
\sectionsymbol{\(\mathcal{W}_{ik}, S_t^{(b)}, \hat{z}_{t|t-1}^{(b)}\)}{trajectory-local window descriptor, model-residual scale, and backend-conditioned prediction}{backend-aware feature metadata}
}

\noindent
The methodology distinguishes feature families because they make different
claims about history, geometry, and transferability. A feature is not only a
column name. It is a declared mapping
\begin{equation}
f_j :
(\tau,\ t,\ \mathcal{W},\ b)
\mapsto
\mathbb{R}^{d_j},
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{W}\) describes the history window and \(b\) is any required
model or backend context. The full feature table for trajectory \(i\) at time
\(k\) is then
\begin{equation}
\phi_{ik}
=
\bigl[
f_1(\tau_i,t_{ik},\mathcal{W}_{ik},b_i),\dots,f_J(\tau_i,t_{ik},\mathcal{W}_{ik},b_i)
\bigr].
\end{equation}

The current taxonomy separates feature families by the kind of evidence they
encode:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{F}
=
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{inst}}
\cup
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{window}}
\cup
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cum}}
\cup
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{robust}}
\cup
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{deriv}}
\cup
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{resid}}.
\end{align}
These are not interchangeable labels. Each family carries a different history
behavior:
\begin{align}
f_j \in \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{inst}}
&\Rightarrow
f_j(\tau,t) = g(z(t)), \\
f_j \in \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{window}}
&\Rightarrow
f_j(\tau,t) = g(z_{W(t)}, t_{W(t)}), \\
f_j \in \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cum}}
&\Rightarrow
f_j(\tau,t_k) = \sum_{r=1}^k g(z_r, \Delta t_r), \\
f_j \in \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{robust}}
&\Rightarrow
f_j(\tau,t) = g_{\mathrm{robust}}(z_{W(t)}), \\
f_j \in \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{deriv}}
&\Rightarrow
f_j(\tau,t) = g\!\left(\frac{\Delta z}{\Delta t}, \frac{\Delta^2 z}{\Delta t^2}, \dots\right), \\
f_j \in \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{resid}}
&\Rightarrow
f_j(\tau,t,b) = g(z_t - \hat{z}_{t|t-1}^{(b)}, S_t^{(b)}).
\end{align}

This classification immediately explains several methodological warnings. If
\(f_j \in \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cum}}\), then successive time steps are not
independent evidence atoms:
\begin{equation}
f_j(\tau,t_{k+1})
\neq
f_j(\tau,t_k) + \text{independent increment}
\quad \text{in general}.
\end{equation}
If \(f_j \in \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{deriv}}\), then irregular sampling perturbs
its numerical stability through \(\Delta t_k\). If
\(f_j \in \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{resid}}\), then the feature inherits the
assumptions of the backend model that produced \(\hat{z}_{t|t-1}\) and \(S_t\).

\subsection*{Sensitivity And Dependence Semantics}
The reason the taxonomy matters is that feature performance is conditional on
which nuisances it amplifies. A useful abstract description is the feature
sensitivity vector
\begin{equation}
\sigma(f_j)
=
\bigl(
s_{\mathrm{noise}}(f_j),
s_{\mathrm{duration}}(f_j),
s_{\mathrm{count}}(f_j),
s_{\mathrm{outlier}}(f_j),
s_{\mathrm{model}}(f_j)
\bigr),
\end{equation}
where each component records how strongly the feature is expected to move under
that nuisance. A raw maximum, for example, tends to have high outlier and
duration sensitivity; a trimmed-range feature has lower outlier sensitivity but
may lose boundary information; an innovation norm has explicit model
sensitivity because its meaning depends on the assumed dynamics and noise.

Dependence also matters because several classifiers use diagonal or naive-Bayes
likelihood approximations. For feature set \(F \subseteq \mathcal{F}\), a
useful warning statistic is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Dep}(F)
=
\frac{1}{|F|(|F|-1)}
\sum_{f_i \neq f_j}
\left|
\mathrm{corr}(f_i, f_j)
\right|,
\end{equation}
or a stronger mutual-information analogue when available. High dependence does
not invalidate the feature set, but it changes how later likelihood models and
importance claims should be interpreted.

\subsection*{Transferability To 3D}
The taxonomy is also the bridge to dimensional lift. A feature should not be
judged by whether its current \(1\text{D}\) formula can be copied verbatim.
It should be judged by whether the underlying geometric idea transfers. The
transfer map is
\begin{equation}
T_{1\mathrm{D}\rightarrow 3\mathrm{D}}(f_j)
\in
\{\text{direct},\ \text{via norm/projection},\ \text{model-specific},\ \text{rewrite required}\}.
\end{equation}
For example, scalar velocity becomes speed \(\|v\|\), signed vertical velocity
becomes \(\langle v, \hat{u}_{\mathrm{up}}\rangle\), and scalar innovation can
be lifted to Mahalanobis innovation norm
\begin{equation}
\|\nu_k\|_{S_k^{-1}}^2 = \nu_k^\top S_k^{-1}\nu_k.
\end{equation}
This is the real feature-taxonomy claim: the repo is trying to declare
\emph{feature principles}, not only a bag of scalar formulas.

\section{Witness Problems}
The repository uses small 1D witness problems as methodological proofs. Their purpose is to isolate one layer of reasoning at a time rather than to claim a finished deployment-grade taxonomy.
\begin{equation}
w_r = (\mathcal{D}_r, f_r, \mathcal{C}_r, m_r, q_r),
\end{equation}
where \(w_r\) is the witness attached to methodology rung \(r\) and \(q_r\) is
the narrow claim that witness is supposed to test. The witness methodology is
therefore closer to a proof obligation than to a benchmark:
\begin{equation}
w_r \models q_r
\qquad \text{means} \qquad
\text{the generated artifacts supply evidence for claim } q_r.
\end{equation}
This is intentionally weaker than saying the witness proves the whole
repository. A witness only needs to isolate one layer cleanly enough that the
success or failure can be interpreted.

The internal structure of a good witness is:
\begin{align}
\text{minimal scenario complexity}
&\land
\text{controlled class contrast}
\land
\text{targeted failure mode}
\notag \\
&\land
\text{interpretable metrics}
\land
\text{artifact traceability}.
\end{align}
That is why the current repo prefers small 1D witnesses over immediately
jumping to 3D mixtures. If the rung fails on a minimal interpretable witness,
adding dimensional complexity would only blur the diagnosis.

\subsection*{Witness Acceptance Logic}
For a witness \(w_r\), let \(\mathcal{M}_r\) denote the metric family tied to
its claim and let \(B_r\) denote the baseline or previous rung. A simplified
acceptance relation is
\begin{equation}
w_r \models q_r
\iff
\mathcal{G}(w_r)=\mathrm{pass}
\land
\Delta \mathcal{M}_r(B_r,m_r) \ge \tau_r
\land
\text{failure mode addressed}(r).
\end{equation}
The last clause matters. A rung is not accepted only because it scores higher.
It is accepted because it addresses the specific weakness the witness was
constructed to expose.

\subsection{Pointwise Evidence Baseline}
The pointwise rung asks the weakest credible classification question:
\begin{quote}
\textit{How far can direct local evidence go without explicit temporal memory or
state dynamics?}
\end{quote}
For class \(c\), let the current observation or local scalar evidence be
\(z_k\). The pointwise baseline assumes
\begin{equation}
z_k \mid c \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_c, \sigma_c^2),
\end{equation}
so the per-step log evidence is
\begin{equation}
\ell_k(c)
=
-\frac{1}{2}
\left[
\log(2\pi \sigma_c^2)
+
\frac{(z_k-\mu_c)^2}{\sigma_c^2}
\right].
\end{equation}
Combined with the shared posterior recursion, this becomes
\begin{equation}
p_k(c) \propto p_{k-1}(c)\exp(\ell_k(c)).
\end{equation}
The pointwise method is therefore already recursive, but the only memory it
retains is the posterior vector itself. It cannot represent trend, persistence,
or switching except through repeated accumulation of independent local scores.

\subsection{Windowed Feature Evidence}
The next rung addresses the failure mode where classes differ only after short
temporal structure is summarized. Let
\begin{equation}
\phi_k = g(z_{1:k}) \qquad \text{or} \qquad \phi_k = g(z_{k-w+1:k}),
\end{equation}
where \(g\) extracts a short-history feature vector and \(w\) is the effective
window. The current repository uses features such as extrema, range, slope,
monotonicity, sign changes, and robust summary variants.

The simplest likelihood factorization is a naive-Bayes approximation across
feature coordinates:
\begin{equation}
p(\phi_k \mid c) \approx \prod_{j=1}^{d_\phi} p(\phi_{k,j} \mid c),
\end{equation}
which yields
\begin{equation}
\ell_k(c)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{d_\phi}
\log \mathcal{N}(\phi_{k,j}; \mu_{c,j}, \sigma_{c,j}^2).
\end{equation}
This rung is stronger than pointwise scoring because the evidence atom is no
longer one raw sample. It is a compressed local geometry summary. Its main risk
is methodological rather than numerical: the features may be under-expressive,
or they may look artificially strong because the corpus makes the task too
easy.

\subsection{Sequential Bayesian Accumulator}
The sequential Bayes rung turns recursive evidence combination into a first-
class algorithm surface. If a provider emits direct likelihoods \(L_k(c)\), the
accumulator writes
\begin{equation}
\log \tilde{p}_k(c)
=
\lambda \log p_{k-1}(c) + \log L_k(c),
\end{equation}
where \(\lambda \in (0,1]\) is the forgetting factor and the normalized
posterior is
\begin{equation}
p_k(c)
=
\frac{\tilde{p}_k(c)}{\sum_{c' \in \mathcal{C}} \tilde{p}_k(c')}.
\end{equation}
When \(L_k(c)\) is itself Gaussian observation likelihood,
\begin{equation}
L_k(c) = \mathcal{N}(z_k; \mu_c, \sigma_c^2),
\end{equation}
the accumulator can be read as the pure recursive shell between local evidence
construction and posterior normalization. This rung improves on pointwise
classification when history matters, but it also amplifies calibration errors:
bad evidence gets accumulated just as efficiently as good evidence.

\subsection{Covariance-Aware State Evidence}
There is a useful intermediate case between pure feature likelihoods and a full
Kalman bank: the classifier may receive a filtered state estimate
\((x_k, P_k)\) from elsewhere. In that case the right evidence atom is not a
new innovation residual but a covariance-aware state-likelihood score:
\begin{equation}
\ell_k(c)
=
-\frac{1}{2}
\left[
(x_k-\mu_c)^\top \Sigma_{k,c}^{-1}(x_k-\mu_c)
+
\log |\Sigma_{k,c}|
+
d_x \log(2\pi)
\right],
\end{equation}
where \(\Sigma_{k,c}\) is the supplied covariance or a class-conditioned
inflated version of it. This rung is conceptually important because it shows
that not every model-based method must rerun tracking internally.

\subsection{Kalman Innovation Bank}
The Kalman bank is the first rung where class evidence is explicitly tied to a
dynamics model rather than only a feature summary. For model \(m\),
\begin{align}
x_k^{(m)} &= F_k^{(m)} x_{k-1}^{(m)} + w_k^{(m)}, \\
z_k &= H_k^{(m)} x_k^{(m)} + v_k^{(m)},
\end{align}
with Gaussian process and measurement noise. The predict step is
\begin{align}
\hat{x}_{k|k-1}^{(m)} &= F_k^{(m)} x_{k-1|k-1}^{(m)}, \\
P_{k|k-1}^{(m)} &= F_k^{(m)} P_{k-1|k-1}^{(m)} {F_k^{(m)}}^\top + Q_k^{(m)}.
\end{align}
The innovation residual and covariance are then
\begin{align}
\nu_k^{(m)} &= z_k - H_k^{(m)} \hat{x}_{k|k-1}^{(m)}, \\
S_k^{(m)} &= H_k^{(m)} P_{k|k-1}^{(m)} {H_k^{(m)}}^\top + R_k^{(m)},
\end{align}
and the evidence increment is the innovation likelihood
\begin{equation}
\ell_k(m)
=
-\frac{1}{2}
\left[
(\nu_k^{(m)})^\top (S_k^{(m)})^{-1}\nu_k^{(m)}
+
\log |S_k^{(m)}|
+
d_z \log(2\pi)
\right].
\end{equation}
This rung addresses endpoint ambiguity and irregular-timing ambiguity when
class differences are about dynamical consistency, not just geometry.

\subsection{Transition-Aware Accumulation}
The transition-matrix rung addresses a different failure mode: the class
identity itself may switch over time. Let \(T_{ij} = p(c_k=j \mid c_{k-1}=i)\).
Before adding new evidence, the prior is propagated by
\begin{equation}
\bar{p}_k(j) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}} T_{ij} p_{k-1}(i).
\end{equation}
The posterior update then becomes
\begin{equation}
\log \tilde{p}_k(j) = \log \bar{p}_k(j) + \ell_k(j).
\end{equation}
In the current implementation the emission score is built from finite-
difference speed and acceleration proxies:
\begin{align}
\log E_k(s)
=\;&
\log \mathcal{N}(\hat{v}_k; \mu_s^{(v)}, {\sigma_s^{(v)}}^2)
\notag \\
&+
\log \mathcal{N}(\hat{a}_k; \mu_s^{(a)}, {\sigma_s^{(a)}}^2)
+
\log \mathcal{N}(|\hat{a}_k|; \mu_s^{(|a|)}, {\sigma_s^{(|a|)}}^2).
\end{align}
This rung proves that explicit switching structure can help before the repo
commits to a full IMM-style multiple-model state estimator.

\subsection{Advanced-Filter Gates}
IMM, PF, and RBPF are treated as algorithm families, but they remain
promotion-gated. Their role in the paper is therefore twofold:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item state the real update equations, and
  \item state the failure evidence required before they are methodologically
  justified.
\end{enumerate}

For IMM, the predicted mode prior is
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mu}_k^{(j)} = \sum_i \Pi_{ij}\mu_{k-1}^{(i)},
\end{equation}
with updated mode posterior
\begin{equation}
\mu_k^{(j)}
=
\frac{\Lambda_k^{(j)}\bar{\mu}_k^{(j)}}{\sum_m \Lambda_k^{(m)}\bar{\mu}_k^{(m)}}.
\end{equation}
For a bootstrap particle filter, the particle weights reduce to
\begin{align}
x_k^{(i)} &\sim p(x_k \mid x_{k-1}^{(i)}, u_k), \\
w_k^{(i)} &\propto w_{k-1}^{(i)} p(z_k \mid x_k^{(i)}, c), \\
N_{\mathrm{eff}} &= \frac{1}{\sum_i (w_k^{(i)})^2}.
\end{align}
For RBPF, the latent split is
\begin{equation}
x_k = (r_k, s_k),
\qquad
p(r_{1:k}, s_k \mid z_{1:k})
=
p(s_k \mid r_{1:k}, z_{1:k}) p(r_{1:k} \mid z_{1:k}),
\end{equation}
so the hard nonlinear or discrete latent variable is sampled while the
conditionally tractable substate remains analytically filtered.

The promotion rule for escalation is not “the method exists.” It is
\begin{equation}
\text{failure}(M_0, \mathcal{D})
\land
\text{capability gain}(M_1)
\land
\text{measurable success criterion}(M_1),
\end{equation}
which is why these advanced methods remain downstream of explicit witness
failures rather than upstream of them.

\subsection{Rung Sufficiency Variables}
The study candidate score is not the same thing as the rung-sufficiency
decision. For study \(s\) at current rung \(r\), the escalation logic defines
several explicit diagnostics:
\begin{align}
g_{\text{oracle}}(s,r)
&=
\max\!\bigl(0, A_{\text{oracle}}(s,r) - A_{\text{current}}(s,r)\bigr), \\
f_{\text{prior}}(s,r)
&=
1 - \text{prior\_sensitivity\_score}(s,r), \\
\Delta_{r \rightarrow r^+}(s)
&=
A_{\text{current}}(s,r^+) - A_{\text{current}}(s,r),
\end{align}
where \(r^+\) is the configured next rung in the capability matrix. These
quantities separate three different reasons a study may fail to promote:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item the evidence geometry is weak, so the current rung is not the problem;
  \item the current rung is underperforming an available oracle opportunity;
  \item a stronger rung exists but has not yet shown a measurable gain.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Learnability Gate}
Before blaming the current algorithm, the repo checks whether the study looks
learnable at all under the active features and corpus. The current learnability
indicator is approximated by
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{1}_{\text{learnable}}(s,r)
=
\mathbf{1}\!\left[
    A_{\text{oracle}} \ge \tau_{\text{oracle}}
    \land
    \mathrm{overlap} \le \tau_{\text{overlap}}
    \land
    \mathrm{AUC}_{\text{pair}} \ge \tau_{\text{AUC}}
    \land
    m_{\text{post}} \ge \tau_{\text{margin}}
\right],
\end{equation}
with current default thresholds
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\text{oracle}} = 0.85,\qquad
\tau_{\text{overlap}} = 0.90,\qquad
\tau_{\text{AUC}} = 0.70,\qquad
\tau_{\text{margin}} = 0.05.
\end{equation}
This is the mathematical form of a key repo rule: feature and corpus limits
should be identified before algorithm blame is assigned.

\subsection{Promotion Rule}
The promotion decision is then a gated piecewise rule rather than a single
accuracy threshold. Let \(\tau_{\text{prior}} = 0.12\),
\(\tau_{\Delta} = 0.05\), and \(\tau_{\text{gap}} = 0.08\). Then the current
decision surface is approximately
\begin{equation}
d(s,r)
=
\begin{cases}
    \texttt{revise\_corpus}, & \neg \text{can\_evaluate\_classifier}(s), \\
    \texttt{revise\_features}, & \text{feature gate fails}, \\
    \texttt{revise\_prior}, & f_{\text{prior}}(s,r) > \tau_{\text{prior}}, \\
    \texttt{feature\_limited}, & \neg \mathbf{1}_{\text{learnable}}(s,r), \\
    \texttt{stay}, & r^+ = \varnothing, \\
    \texttt{reject\_escalation}, & \neg \text{capability\_match}(r,r^+), \\
    \texttt{defer\_advanced}, & \Delta_{r \rightarrow r^+}(s)\ \text{is unavailable}, \\
    \texttt{promote}, & \Delta_{r \rightarrow r^+}(s) \ge \tau_{\Delta}, \\
    \texttt{stay}, & g_{\text{oracle}}(s,r) < \tau_{\text{gap}}, \\
    \texttt{defer\_advanced}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
This rule is central to the methodology claim. It is the reason the repository
can say “do not escalate complexity just because a stronger method exists.”

\subsection{Decision Vocabulary}
At the study-report level, the final terminal vocabulary is intentionally
smaller than the internal rung-sufficiency labels:
\begin{equation}
d_{\text{final}} \in \{\texttt{promote}, \texttt{revise}, \texttt{reject}, \texttt{defer}\}.
\end{equation}
These mean:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \texttt{promote}: the current evidence and rung are strong enough to move forward;
  \item \texttt{revise}: a localized corpus, feature, prior, or ladder issue remains;
  \item \texttt{reject}: the declared study claim is not currently supported;
  \item \texttt{defer}: the required capability is unimplemented or not yet justified.
\end{itemize}
This vocabulary is intentionally conservative. A visually interesting study is
not enough to justify \texttt{promote}; the corpus, feature, posterior, and
failure-mode stories all need to agree.

\section{Stage V Evaluate: Calibration And Posterior Quality}
\paragraph{Section Symbols.}
\sectionsymbols{
\sectionsymbol{\(\mathrm{Brier}\)}{multiclass Brier score}{quadratic posterior error metric}
\sectionsymbol{\(\mathrm{ECE}\)}{expected calibration error}{bin-based confidence calibration diagnostic}
\sectionsymbol{\(H_t\)}{posterior entropy at time \(t\)}{decisiveness or uncertainty diagnostic}
\sectionsymbol{\(I_b\)}{confidence bin \(b\)}{group used for empirical calibration summaries}
}

\noindent
Posterior quality is not only endpoint accuracy. The current methodology also cares about calibration and probabilistic sharpness. Two standard metrics are
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Brier}
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^N
\sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}}
\left(
p_i(c)-\mathbf{1}[y_i=c]
\right)^2
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECE}
=
\sum_{b=1}^B
\frac{|I_b|}{N}
\left|
\mathrm{acc}(I_b)-\mathrm{conf}(I_b)
\right|.
\end{equation}
Posterior entropy is the complementary uncertainty diagnostic:
\begin{equation}
H_t = -\sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} p_t(c)\log p_t(c).
\end{equation}
Low entropy means the posterior is decisive. That is only a good sign when the decisive class is also correct; otherwise the method is confidently wrong rather than well calibrated.

The current accumulator calibration bins make the distinction concrete. In the
highest-confidence bin, the repository currently reports 175 cases with
accuracy \(0.96\), mean confidence about \(0.9993\), and a gap of about
\(0.0393\). In the sparse \(0.4\)--\(0.5\) bin, it reports 7 cases with
accuracy \(0.0\), mean confidence \(0.5\), and a gap of \(0.5\). That is a
strong high-confidence regime with a weak ambiguous regime.

The current shared-corpus comparison summarizes the method-level picture:
\begin{center}
\small

\toprule
Method & Overall & Prior flip fraction & Interpretation \\
\midrule
Pointwise & 0.875 & 0.479 & Useful lower bound, but brittle to prior shifts \\
Windowed (raw) & 0.750 & 0.021 & Stable under priors, but not separative enough \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The point of these numbers is not that low prior sensitivity is always better. The point is that posterior quality is a bundle of properties:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item accuracy: does the top class match the truth?
  \item calibration: does the reported probability mean what it says?
  \item entropy: is the posterior decisive or diffuse?
  \item prior robustness: does a small prior perturbation flip the answer?
\end{itemize}
The current evidence says the accumulator is the best end-to-end combiner on this corpus, pointwise is a useful sanity check, robust windowing buys stability without improving separation, and the velocity-aided Kalman bank helps when the extra sensor channel is actually available.

\section{Stage IV Audit: Class Validity And Relabel Logic}
\paragraph{Section Symbols.}
\sectionsymbols{
\sectionsymbol{\(v_c(\tau)\)}{validity score assigned to class \(c\) for trajectory \(\tau\)}{audits whether the requested label is plausible}
\sectionsymbol{\(\hat{c}_{\mathrm{valid}}(\tau)\)}{highest-validity class under the audit logic}{candidate relabel target}

\noindent
The corpus explorer does not blindly trust a requested class label. For trajectory \(\tau\), each class receives a validity score
\begin{equation}
v_c(\tau) \in [0,1],
\qquad
\hat{c}_{\mathrm{valid}}(\tau) = \arg\max_c v_c(\tau).
\end{equation}
The current label-status logic can be summarized as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{label\_status}(\tau)=
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{valid\ target}, & v_{c_{\mathrm{target}}}(\tau)\ge \alpha, \\
\mathrm{ambiguous}, & \max_c v_c(\tau)-\max_{c \neq c^*} v_c(\tau) < \delta, \\
\mathrm{relabel\ candidate}, & \hat{c}_{\mathrm{valid}}(\tau)\neq c_{\mathrm{target}}, \\
\mathrm{invalid}, & \max_c v_c(\tau) < \beta.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
This is the formal bridge from requested target class to auditable generated labels.

The current ladder proves the utility of pointwise, windowed, sequential Bayes, Kalman-bank, transition-matrix, and now an explicit IMM proof surface. The key policy claim is that advanced methods are downstream of failure evidence. If \(M_0\) is the current simpler method and \(M_1\) is a more advanced filter family, the repository is trying to justify a transition only when
\begin{equation}
\text{failure}(M_0, \mathcal{D}) \land \text{capability gain}(M_1) \land \text{measurable success criterion}(M_1)
\end{equation}
can all be stated explicitly. That is why the advanced-filter reports are decision artifacts rather than implementation plans by default.

For IMM specifically, the current proof surface follows the standard
switching-state recursion. If \(\mu_{k-1}^{(i)}\) is the posterior for mode
\(i\) and \(\Pi_{ij}\) is the transition probability into mode \(j\), then the
predicted mode prior is
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mu}_{k}^{(j)}
=
\sum_i \Pi_{ij}\mu_{k-1}^{(i)},
\end{equation}
with mixing weights
\begin{equation}
\omega_{ij}^{(k)}
=
\frac{\Pi_{ij}\mu_{k-1}^{(i)}}{\bar{\mu}_{k}^{(j)}}.
\end{equation}
The mixed state and covariance for target mode \(j\) are then formed before the
mode-specific Kalman predict/update step, each mode emits an innovation
likelihood \(\Lambda_k^{(j)}\), and the updated mode posterior becomes
\begin{equation}
\mu_k^{(j)}
=
\frac{\Lambda_k^{(j)}\bar{\mu}_k^{(j)}}{\sum_m \Lambda_k^{(m)}\bar{\mu}_k^{(m)}}.
\end{equation}
This is stronger than the transition-matrix accumulator because IMM mixes full
mode-conditioned latent-state distributions rather than only label probability
mass.

For completeness, the deferred advanced-filter families fit the same evidence story. A particle filter starts from a proposal distribution
\begin{equation}
x_k^{(i)} \sim q(x_k \mid x_{0:k-1}^{(i)}, z_{1:k}),
\end{equation}
with unnormalized importance weights
\begin{equation}
\tilde{w}_k^{(i)}
=
w_{k-1}^{(i)}
\frac{
    p(z_k \mid x_k^{(i)})\,p(x_k^{(i)} \mid x_{k-1}^{(i)})
}{
    q(x_k^{(i)} \mid x_{0:k-1}^{(i)}, z_{1:k})
}.
\end{equation}
For the bootstrap choice \(q = p(x_k \mid x_{k-1})\), this reduces to
\begin{align}
x_k^{(i)} &\sim p(x_k \mid x_{k-1}^{(i)}, u_k), \\
w_k^{(i)} &\propto w_{k-1}^{(i)} p(z_k \mid x_k^{(i)}, c), \\
\hat{p}(z_k \mid c) &\approx \sum_i w_{k-1}^{(i)} p(z_k \mid x_k^{(i)}, c),
\end{align}
followed by normalization and, when necessary, resampling triggered by
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{1}{\sum_i (w_k^{(i)})^2}.
\end{equation}
PF therefore fits the repo only if the particle cloud can still emit
class-conditioned predictive evidence \(\hat{p}(z_k \mid c)\) for the shared
posterior update.

The current gate-to-algorithm example is still a no-go: the advanced-filter

while an RBPF decomposition writes \(x_k = (r_k, s_k)\) and factors

p(r_{1:k}, s_k \mid z_{1:k})
=
p(s_k \mid r_{1:k}, z_{1:k}) p(r_{1:k} \mid z_{1:k}).

RBPF is only justified when part of the latent state remains conditionally tractable given sampled nonlinear or discrete structure.

\subsection{Escalation Criteria By Rung}
The advanced-filter gate becomes concrete only when stated against the simpler
rungs it may replace. The current escalation logic is:
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\text{kalman} \rightarrow \text{transition}}
&=
A_{\text{post-switch}}^{\text{transition}}
-
A_{\text{post-switch}}^{\text{kalman}}, \\
\Delta_{\text{transition} \rightarrow \text{IMM}}
&=
A_{\text{post-switch}}^{\text{IMM}}
-
A_{\text{post-switch}}^{\text{transition}}.
\end{align}
The interpretation is:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item if \(\Delta_{\text{kalman} \rightarrow \text{transition}}\) is material, switching-aware probability propagation is justified;
  \item if \(\Delta_{\text{transition} \rightarrow \text{IMM}}\) is material, mixing state distributions is justified beyond label-only switching;
  \item if robust Kalman and transition-aware methods still explain the witness, PF should remain deferred;
  \item if there is no true sampled-latent plus analytic-substate split, RBPF should remain deferred.
\end{itemize}
This is how the decision layer prevents the repo from treating IMM, PF, or
RBPF as default prestige methods instead of evidence-earned rungs.

For trajectory \(i\), time index \(k\), and class \(c\), the common prediction
record is
\begin{equation}
r_{i,k}
=
(\mathrm{trajectory\_id}_i,\ t_k,\ y_i^\star,\ \hat{y}_{i,k},\ \gamma_{i,k},\ p_{i,k}(c)_{c \in \mathcal{C}},\ \ell_{i,k}(c)_{c \in \mathcal{C}}),
\end{equation}
where \(y_i^\star\) is the true class, \(\hat{y}_{i,k}\) is the predicted
class, \(\gamma_{i,k} = \max_c p_{i,k}(c)\) is the reported confidence,
\(p_{i,k}(c)\) is the posterior, and \(\ell_{i,k}(c)\) is the emitted
log-likelihood increment or equivalent evidence score. Filtered methods may
emit extra state summaries,
\begin{equation}
f_{i,k}
=
(\hat{x}_{i,k},\ P_{i,k},\ \nu_{i,k},\ S_{i,k},\ d_{i,k}),
\end{equation}
where \(d_{i,k}\) is a method-specific diagnostic such as
\(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\), a resampling indicator, or a mode posterior.

The key methodological point is that the metric layer only requires a stable
projection of these records. Endpoint accuracy, calibration, prior
sensitivity, confusion, delay, and pairwise failure analysis consume
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}
=
\left\{
    r_{i,k}
\right\}_{i,k}
\cup
\left\{
    f_{i,k}
\right\}_{i,k},
\end{equation}
not the internal implementation details of the evidence provider. In other
words,
\begin{equation}
\text{method implementation}
\rightarrow
\text{standard artifact rows}
\rightarrow
\text{shared metrics and reports}.
\end{equation}
This is the formal reason the methodology can plausibly survive a later
transition from scalar \(1\text{D}\) witness problems to vector-valued \(3\text{D}\)
corpora.

\subsection{Metric Consumption Of The Contract}
Once the shared rows exist, the evaluation layer becomes method-agnostic. For
example, final-step accuracy is
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathrm{final}}
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^N
\mathbf{1}\!\left[
    \hat{y}_{i,T_i} = y_i^\star
\right],
\end{equation}
negative log-likelihood is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NLL}
=
-\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^N
\log p_{i,T_i}(y_i^\star),
\end{equation}
and confidence-gated error is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Err}_{\gamma \ge \tau}
=
\frac{
\sum_i \mathbf{1}[\gamma_{i,T_i} \ge \tau]
\mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_{i,T_i} \neq y_i^\star]
}{
\sum_i \mathbf{1}[\gamma_{i,T_i} \ge \tau]
}.
\end{equation}
The same rows also support temporal metrics such as time-to-confidence,
post-switch delay, and posterior-area diagnostics. This is why a classifier
comparison report can be generated from one schema family even when one method
is feature-based and another is model-based.

\subsection{Why The Contract Matters For Promotion}
Promotion decisions are not attached to raw implementation names. They are
attached to what the method emits under the common contract. A rung earns
promotion only if its standardized artifacts show a real gain in the
interpreted metrics:
\begin{equation}
\text{promote}(r \rightarrow r^+)
\Rightarrow
\Delta \mathcal{M}(r,r^+) > 0
\quad \text{under the same artifact definitions},
\end{equation}
where \(\Delta \mathcal{M}\) may refer to accuracy, NLL, calibration,
switch-delay, or prior-robustness improvements. Without this contract, each new
method would require a custom report surface, and architectural comparability
would collapse.

\noindent
The synthesis paper should not leave the reader with the impression that a top-line accuracy number is the whole story. The intended interpretation checklist is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Did the corpus pass adequacy?} If not, do not trust leaderboard-style claims yet.
  \item \textbf{Are the class labels valid?} If not, fix class definitions, relabel logic, or corpus generation before blaming the classifier.
  \item \textbf{Are the active features excited?} If not, the study is under-exercised and unsupported feature claims should be removed or regenerated.
  \item \textbf{Are the classes separable by the oracle or feature geometry?} If not, the issue is upstream in features, classes, or corpus construction.
  \item \textbf{Does the classifier underperform the oracle?} If yes, the issue is in evidence modeling, posterior updating, or method assumptions.
  \item \textbf{Are the decisions prior-sensitive?} If yes, the result should be reported as fragile rather than robust.
  \item \textbf{Is the confusion localized?} If yes, target the failing class pair, time phase, or regime instead of flattening the result into one global number.
  \item \textbf{Does a more advanced method address a demonstrated failure?} If not, do not escalate the ladder just because a more complex backend exists.
\end{enumerate}

The same checklist can be written as a compact decision flow:
\begin{align}
\neg \text{adequate corpus}
&\Rightarrow
\text{stop and repair corpus},
\notag \\
\text{adequate corpus} \land \neg \text{valid labels}
&\Rightarrow
\text{repair class validity},
\notag \\
\text{adequate corpus} \land \text{valid labels} \land \neg \text{feature excitation}
&\Rightarrow
\text{regenerate or restate feature claim},
\notag \\
\text{adequate corpus} \land \text{valid labels} \land \text{feature excitation} \land \neg \text{oracle separability}
&\Rightarrow
\text{feature/corpus/class-definition issue},
\notag \\
\text{oracle separability} \land \text{classifier underperformance}
&\Rightarrow
\text{algorithm or posterior issue},
\notag \\
\text{good accuracy} \land \text{high prior fragility}
&\Rightarrow
\text{report fragility, not only success}.
\end{align}

\section{Results Summary}
The current artifact stack proves the methodology process end to end: study
candidates can be declared, screened statically, evaluated against generated
corpora, explained through posterior and prior-sensitivity surfaces, and
assigned promotion decisions. More importantly, those steps are no longer
separate scripts. They now form a stable workflow in which corpus design,
feature geometry, evidence construction, posterior updating, evaluation, and
packaging all contribute evidence to one decision surface.

The strongest current result is therefore architectural rather than
leaderboard-oriented. On the shared \(1\text{D}\) witness corpora, the
accumulator is the strongest current end-to-end combiner, but the deeper claim
is that pointwise, windowed, model-based, and switching-aware methods can all
be compared through one artifact family. The project is no longer only asking
``which classifier wins?'' It is asking ``what evidence was available, how was
it accumulated, what failed, and what does that imply about the next rung?''

The present limitations are equally explicit. Scalar witness problems still
dominate the implementation; some corpora still exhibit adequacy and leakage
issues; and advanced filters remain justified only on gated witness problems
rather than by default. Those limitations do not invalidate the current
methodology result. They define the boundary of the current proof.

The most compact mathematical summary of the current contribution is
\begin{equation}
\text{corpus}
\rightarrow
\text{features / evidence}
\rightarrow
\text{posterior}
\rightarrow
\text{evaluation}
\rightarrow
\text{promotion},
\end{equation}
with every arrow now carrying inspectable artifacts. In other words, the
repository has progressed from isolated demonstrations to a partially proven
study calculus. The present 1D stack is not important only because it gives one
set of scores. It is important because it shows that the downstream logic still
works when the upstream evidence atom changes from observations, to features,
to innovations, to switching-aware updates.

The substantive conclusion is therefore conditional but strong:
\begin{quote}
\textit{the repo has not yet proved universal kinematic classification, but it
has proved a reusable methodology for declaring, stressing, comparing, and
judging classification studies across a meaningful algorithm ladder.}
\end{quote}
That is the right level of claim for the current state of evidence.

\section{3D Transition Status}
The current methodology layer distinguishes three statuses:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \codepath{dimension_agnostic}
  \item \codepath{adapter_compatible}
  \item \codepath{rewrite_required}
\end{itemize}
Contracts, shared evaluation, posterior updating, promotion logic, and
artifact packaging are already close to the first category. Scalar feature
extraction, scalar plotting assumptions, and scalar state-space filtering
remain in the latter two categories, so current 3D readiness is methodological
rather than dynamical.

The intended \(3\text{D}\) lift is therefore not
\begin{equation}
\text{rewrite everything},
\end{equation}
but rather the following controlled substitutions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item add a new corpus adapter,
  \item add new feature geometry,
  \item add new dynamics and measurement models,
  \item reuse the shared inference and reporting machinery.
\end{itemize}
In practical terms, the thesis claim is that the difficult reusable part is the
evidence-to-posterior-to-report pipeline, not the current scalar witness
equations. A \(3\text{D}\) upgrade should add vector-valued corpus adapters,
vector-compatible features, model families, and new visualizations while
preserving the study harness and interpretation logic.

\section{Current Status And Next Work}
The current common-study evidence surface combines proxy and direct-likelihood
forms where available. The \(1\text{D}\) studies are witness problems that
anchor the methodology layer, and calibration remains a first-class target for
future bundles. The next steps are now clearer than they were at the start of
this document:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]

  \item tighten the corpus adequacy and leakage gates so classifier claims rest on harder data;
